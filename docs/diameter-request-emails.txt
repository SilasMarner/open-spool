Hi,

That's a fair point — and honestly refreshing to hear from a manufacturer.
You're right that a single "stated diameter" on hollow core is mostly a
guesstimate.

For what I'm building, I don't actually need a true round-cross-section
diameter. The tool only estimates how many yards of a line will fit on a
given reel, so all I really need is a consistent, repeatable measure of how
much spool space the line takes up per length. A few ways that would work,
in order of what's easiest for you:

1. A nominal diameter measured the way you'd spool it — relaxed / light
   spooling tension, not under fighting load. To your question: spooling
   (limp) tension is the state that matters for capacity, since that's how
   the line actually packs on the reel — not 50% or full load. Even labeled
   "approximate, as spooled," it's useful, because my app treats every
   number as an estimate and tells the angler to confirm on the reel.

2. If diameter feels too squishy to commit to, a linear density would be
   even better and is fully repeatable — grams per 100 m, yards per pound,
   or the denier of the braid. That's a clean, quantifiable number and it
   maps directly to how much fills a spool.

3. Or, if you have any real-world capacity data ("X yards of 65 lb fills
   a [reel model]"), even one data point per line lets me calibrate the
   whole size range.

Any one of those three gets me there. I'd genuinely rather publish your
honest "approximate, at spooling tension" figure than the guesstimates
everyone else lists.

Thanks for taking the time —
Matt
